## 1. The grip

Place the base knuckle of your index finger on bevel 3 for an **eastern** grip, or bevel 4 for **semi-western**. Eastern is easier to learn; semi-western makes topspin more natural on higher balls.

## 2. Ready position and unit turn

Split step as your opponent hits. As soon as you read a forehand, turn your shoulders and hips together, with the non-hitting hand pointing toward the ball.

## 3. Swing path

Drop the racket head below the ball, then swing up and through. The low-to-high path lifts the ball over the net and brings it down with topspin.

## 4. Contact

Meet the ball in front of your body, around waist height, with the strings facing your target. Keep your head still through contact.

## 5. Follow-through

Let the racket wrap over your opposite shoulder and recover to the ready position straight away.

## Common mistakes

- **Late preparation.** Turn before the bounce, not after it.
- **Contact too close to the body.** Move your feet so the ball stays in front.
- **Arm-only swing.** Rotate the hips and shoulders to power the shot.

## Drills to practise

1. **Drop-feed forehands.** A partner drops balls beside you; focus on the turn and the contact point.
2. **Cross-court rally.** Rally forehand to forehand, aiming deep past the service line.
3. **Shadow swings.** Swing without a ball in front of a mirror to check the shape.
